Space Gun 32X – Dev diary, 14 October

October 14, 2026

Today’s big fight was speed. Stage 1 kept dropping to about 12 frames a second, so I built a stopwatch into the game to see where each frame went. The first fix was teamwork: the 32X has two processors, and one was doing most of the drawing while the other sat waiting. Now they share the screen fairly, adjusting every frame. Next, the stopwatch showed almost all the remaining time went on painting the 3D corridor walls, one pixel at a time. Clever tricks that looked good on paper made no difference, so I went old school and wrote the innermost drawing loops by hand in the processor’s own language. The walls now draw a quarter faster and the aliens a little faster too. The busiest moments went from 12 to around 20 frames a second, and the quieter stretches run at the full 30.

Then a new idea: the Sega Saturn. It has a chip that stretches sprites by itself, exactly the job that’s been slowing the 32X down, and it plays CDs, so cartridge space stops being a worry. Everything my sprite tool knows about the arcade carries straight over, so I’ve added a switch to it to pick the 32X or the Saturn, and planned the Saturn version step by step. The 32X version comes first and won’t be touched by any of it.

Space Gun 32X – Dev diary, 13 October

October 13, 2026

Today was all about making the aliens behave like the arcade. I recorded a fresh, clean playthrough of stage 1 in MAME and rebuilt the level from it. That cleared up aliens that appeared twice and fixed some missing doors and wall panels. Next I played the arcade again without firing a shot, to see what an alien does when you miss it. It turns out the corridor keeps moving, so a missed alien hits you a few times and then slides off the edge of the screen. The robot arms fire once and pull back up into the ceiling. Those rules are now in the 32X version, along with the arcade’s “stand-still” near the end of the stage, where the corridor won’t move on until every alien is dead. Aliens you haven’t hit no longer twitch as if they’ve been shot; they keep up their own walk-and-attack animation instead. Next up: the breakable windows, and finding room on the cartridge, which is now 3.8 MB of a possible 4 MB.
